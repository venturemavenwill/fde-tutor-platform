Module M17 / Stage 9

Momentum, and Why It Decays

Momentum is not a mood. It is a set of conditions that can stop holding at different times.

Before you read

Consider a change that appeared adopted while a supplier or project team was present. Name the first condition that could disappear after they leave and the first observable sign.

Write the remedy that earns your organization nothing

Record the complete customer-owned remedy before drafting or improving a paid reinforcement proposal. The stronger comparison criterion remains locked until that response is durably recorded.

The durable idea

A condition can change without negligence: attention moves, pain fades, expertise becomes unreachable, authority proves insufficient, or a workload changes shape. A useful reinforcement design names one condition, who holds it, what its failure looks like, and what happens when the trigger fires.

Name the condition that can fail

  • Attention: the question that forced the work into the room disappears.
  • Pain: the old path becomes convenient enough to return.
  • Reachability: small decisions stall because the builders are no longer available.
  • Workload: the assumptions behind the design no longer match real behavior.
  • Authority: the named owner cannot keep the mechanism alive.

Keep three platform claims separate

Permission to request capacity, deployable availability, and a commercial commitment are different claims. None proves either of the others. Current values and product mechanics require separately verified evidence; the durable lesson is to make the distinction before acting.

A mechanism survives observation

A reinforcement design names a customer-owned condition, a cadence that already has a home, an artifact produced without supplier attention, and an observable trigger with a configured response. A ceremony that runs only while an outsider watches is not transfer.

Use a drift register

Record the dated original assumption, the current observation, the input that moved, the decision that moved it, who holds the condition, the first visible sign of failure, and the fallback. Add the conflict-of-interest statement where a reader will encounter it—not as an afterthought.
