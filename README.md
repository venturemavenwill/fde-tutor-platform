# FDE Tutor Platform

**Learn how to carry difficult customer work from first qualification to
lasting, customer-owned capability.**

The FDE Tutor Platform is a learning environment for Forward Deployed
Engineers: people who work where software engineering, product judgment,
delivery, and customer outcomes meet.

It is being built around the
[FDE Playbook](https://fdeplaybook.vercel.app/), a practical curriculum that
covers the full engagement arc in 90 sessions and 90 supporting whitepapers.
The platform adds a persistent learning record, guided practice, spaced
retrieval, authentic work, and bounded AI support so that the material is not
merely read—it can be recalled and used when an engagement becomes difficult.

## Why learn these materials?

Forward-deployed work is rarely difficult because nobody can write code. It is
difficult because the work begins with ambiguity and must survive contact with
real organizations, constraints, incentives, and production systems.

Strong FDEs must be able to:

- Decide whether an opportunity is worth pursuing and what success would mean.
- Discover the real problem rather than automate the first request.
- Separate facts, assumptions, constraints, and commitments.
- Frame an outcome that technical and customer teams can act on together.
- Test the riskiest belief before committing to a large build.
- Deliver systems that are operable, observable, secure, and recoverable.
- Transfer ownership so progress does not depend on the FDE remaining present.
- Create mechanisms that keep producing value after the initial engagement.

These are forms of professional judgment. They are hard to acquire through
documentation, product training, or passive observation alone. The Playbook
makes the methods explicit; the Tutor Platform is designed to turn those methods
into durable practice.

## The engagement arc

The curriculum follows one customer engagement from qualification through
compounding impact:

| Stage | What you learn to do |
|---|---|
| **1. Qualify / Discover** | Recognize the right opportunity, the customer context, and the conditions under which an FDE can help. |
| **2. Discover / Define** | Find the underlying need, evidence, actors, constraints, and decision boundary. |
| **3. Frame / Decompose** | Turn ambiguity into a shared problem frame and a tractable sequence of work. |
| **4. Prove / Plan** | Test the highest-risk assumptions and define the smallest credible path forward. |
| **5. Build / Implement** | Build with the customer while preserving learning, evidence, and adaptability. |
| **6. Assure / Review** | Challenge whether the solution is ready, safe, supportable, and tied to the intended outcome. |
| **7. Operate / Deliver** | Learn from real behavior, degradation, and operational evidence rather than the happy path. |
| **8. Transfer / Operate** | Move capability, decision rights, and operating mechanisms into customer ownership. |
| **9. Compound** | Reinforce the change so value persists and grows after the initial intervention. |

The Transform Guide provides the whole-task journey. The deeper HVE curriculum
provides the concepts, methods, failure modes, exercises, and evidence behind
each stage.

## How the learning experience works

The material is deliberately effortful. A dense professional curriculum can
feel familiar after one reading while leaving very little available at the
moment of need. The platform therefore asks learners to do more than consume
pages.

A typical learning loop asks you to:

1. **Orient** — see the purpose, vocabulary, expected output, and honest time
   commitment.
2. **Retrieve first** — recall prerequisite ideas before reopening them.
3. **Commit to an answer** — make your current thinking visible before reading
   the practitioner model.
4. **Study the material** — work through the concept, method, examples, and
   failure modes.
5. **Recall with the source closed** — reproduce the idea instead of recognizing
   familiar wording.
6. **Compare and revise** — identify the difference between your answer and the
   grounded criterion or model.
7. **Use it on real work** — apply the method to an engagement artifact you
   actually own.
8. **Return later** — retrieve the idea again after time and in a changed
   context.

The governing rule is simple:

> **Where a summary would go, put a question.**

Being shown a good answer can create fluency and confidence. Producing,
comparing, revising, and later retrieving an answer creates evidence that the
idea is becoming usable.

## What the Tutor Platform adds

The existing Playbook already contains the curriculum and its learning design.
The Tutor Platform extends it with:

- **Continuity across sessions and devices** — your attempts and revisions do
  not disappear when you leave a page.
- **A transparent evidence record** — recall, explanation, discrimination,
  transfer, independence, and support used remain distinct.
- **A meaningful next step** — return to due practice or missing evidence, not
  simply the next page.
- **Bounded AI coaching** — the tutor can elicit, question, cue, contrast, and
  ask for revision without immediately giving away the answer.
- **Grounded guidance** — substantive answers retain their source and content
  revision.
- **Authentic practice** — the goal is better work on a live engagement, not a
  higher engagement score.
- **Human review where it matters** — consequential or uncertain judgments
  remain inspectable, contestable, and reviewable.

The result is not a chatbot placed beside a course. It is a learning system with
memory, explicit teaching policy, and evidence of what the learner can do
without support.

## What this platform will not do

The FDE Tutor Platform is not designed to:

- Replace the Playbook with AI-generated curriculum.
- Reward page views, streaks, chat volume, or time on platform as if they were
  learning.
- Treat course completion as professional competence.
- Collapse complex judgment into a percentage or opaque mastery score.
- Reveal model answers before a genuine attempt.
- Let an AI make irreversible, high-consequence decisions about a learner.
- Assess facts that have gone stale, such as current prices, quotas, ratios, or
  regional availability.

The standard is delayed, unaided use in authentic work—not whether the material
felt easy to read.

## The first learning slice

The first platform slice is **S083**, a session about designing reinforcement
that continues without supplier presence.

Learners must first propose an unpaid remedy before seeing the stronger
criterion or improving a paid proposal. The exercise then sharpens four
questions:

- Is there an owner employed by the customer?
- Is the cadence sustainable without the supplier?
- Is an artifact produced whether or not anyone is watching?
- Is the trigger an observable state change rather than a person remembering?

S083 is intentionally not used to award mastery. It is the first complete test
of the platform's learning loop: attempt before reveal, explicit comparison,
revision, authentic application, durable state, and later retrieval.

## Who is this for?

The material is primarily for:

- Forward Deployed Engineers and people preparing for the role.
- Engineers moving from feature delivery into customer-facing problem solving.
- Technical and product leaders responsible for complex customer outcomes.
- Coaches and reviewers helping practitioners improve judgment, not just
  activity or output.

Prior experience helps, but the curriculum does not assume that experience
automatically produces a reusable method. It asks experienced practitioners to
make their reasoning explicit and gives newer practitioners a structured way to
build it.
